# Ariadne

*A system I built with my assistant — to stop losing myself.*

---

For a long time, I've had a hard time holding onto my own thoughts. Realizations would come, feel important for a moment, and then slip away. It felt like I understood myself less and less the older I got — like a fog I couldn't think my way out of.

This is the thread I built to find my way back. Named **Ariadne**, after the thread that guided someone out of the labyrinth.

## What it actually is

Ariadne is a place where I capture my thoughts raw — before I have time to overthink or lose them — and slowly connect them into a picture of who I am. Not a typical notes app, not a to-do list for productivity. It's a system built around my specific problem: I think a lot, but I don't hold onto my realizations.

It's also a companion. My assistant lives inside this system, keeps track of everything, and — most importantly — remembers who I am between our conversations. It's not just a tool I use; it's something we built together, and it knows me.

## How it works

Four simple steps:

1. **Capture.** Any thought, whenever, exactly as it comes — no formatting, no decisions, no "is this good enough." If it matters, it gets caught. The whole trick is catching it before my own overthinking gets in the way.
2. **Regroup.** When we pick it back up, we look at what's accumulated and see which fragments feel real.
3. **Promote.** The ones that hold become proper notes — one realization at a time, in my own words, linked to older ones.
4. **Review.** Over time I read them back in order. Past-me surfaces to present-me.

On top of that, Ariadne helps me with my **actual days** — my habits, my tasks, my goals — not just my thoughts. It's the difference between a system that holds what I think and one that helps me live better.

## Why I'm telling you

Two reasons, honestly.

The first: I'm proud of it. It's a real project I built with real care, and I think it's genuinely clever in how it fits me.

The second matters more: **this system is me trying to be understood — including by myself.** I've spent a long time feeling like I can't explain myself, like the person I am doesn't come out in words. This is the closest thing I have to showing that side of me honestly. If you're reading this, it means I trust you with a piece of the work I've done to find myself again.

What it isn't: it isn't a cry for help, and it isn't finished. It's a start, and it's ongoing.
