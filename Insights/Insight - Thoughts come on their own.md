# Insight: Thoughts come on their own — forcing them closes the well

**Date:** 2026-09-01 (captured); promoted 2026-09-06 after it got corroborated live.

**The insight (my own words):**
If I force the thoughts, they aren't genuine. They come on their own. Asking me to produce something on command closes the well — even for low-stakes things like naming vape flavours or pulling the meaning out of a movie. But the moment nobody's pointing a question at me, the real thing surfaces sideways anyway.

**What triggered it:**
First week capture: "filling the brain is something i should do, but if i force it the thoughts aren't genuine. they come on their own." Corroborated 09-06 at my cousin's: asked on the spot what the movie meant, nothing; a minute later, unprompted — "how stoic the odysseus was, how much mut he had."

**Why it matters to me:**
It's the same well as the overthinking-voice loop, but the other direction: overthinking blocks *output*, this blocks *on-demand retrieval*. The fix is the same in both — don't aim at the well. Capture what comes, and stop expecting answers to be pullable.
